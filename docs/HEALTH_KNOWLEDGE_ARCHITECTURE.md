# NISAA CARE Health Knowledge Architecture

Health knowledge is a governed product layer, not filler copy.

Every article should carry a stable content ID, topic, title/body, language, source publisher/title/URL, publication or review date when known, evidence/source class, review state, content version and last-verified timestamp.

## Knowledge map

### Menstrual foundations
- What menstruation is
- What a menstrual cycle is
- How cycle days are counted
- Typical adult cycle range
- Typical period duration
- Flow and symptom logging
- Cycle variability
- First-period education
- Menstrual hygiene and dignity

### Symptoms and wellbeing
- Period pain
- Cramps
- Bloating
- Headache
- Mood changes
- Fatigue
- Sleep changes
- Gastrointestinal symptoms
- Symptoms that disrupt daily activities

### Pattern review
- Bleeding longer than 7 days
- Cycles outside the common 21–35-day adult range
- Heavy bleeding observations
- Absent or infrequent periods
- Severe or worsening pain
- Symptoms persisting outside expected period context

These are educational/care-review signals, not diagnoses.

### Fertility education
- Ovulation as a biological process
- Fertile-window estimation limitations
- Why estimates vary
- Why irregular cycles reduce confidence
- TTC tracking
- Why estimates cannot be represented as guaranteed contraception

### Conditions as education
Endometriosis, adenomyosis, fibroids, PMS/PMDD and amenorrhea can be explained as educational topics with source attribution. NISAA CARE must never infer a condition from a symptom alone.

WHO currently describes the menstrual cycle as averaging 21–35 days and emphasizes accurate information, dignity, access to care and attention to worrisome symptoms. ACOG describes a typical adult cycle as 21–35 days and a typical period as up to 7 days. These are population-level educational references, not individual diagnoses.

## Governance

external source -> normalize -> editorial review -> REVIEW_REQUIRED -> human verification -> VERIFIED -> PUBLISHED -> signed/versioned package.

Translations are independently reviewed. Arabic RTL presentation is UI; translation correctness is content governance.

## UX

Health -> Learn -> Your cycle -> Symptoms -> Fertility education -> Care signals -> Sources.

Every article exposes:
- What this means
- What NISAA CARE can/cannot tell you
- When to consider professional care
- Source and last reviewed
